LAN 121 — Network Essentials

This course introduces the operation of computer networks and networking devices, with an examination of the evolution of data communications and computer networks. Hardware and software network configurations, operations, and requirements are discussed — including network media, physical and logical topologies, the OSI model, protocols and standards, network implementation, and network support. This course is designed to prepare the successful student for the CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) certification.

OSI Model · Appliances · Cloud · SDN/SD-WAN · Zero Trust

Network Operations II — Management, Monitoring & Disaster Recovery

Documentation · Change Mgmt · SNMP & Flow Data · DR · VPNs

Processes and procedures: documentation (physical vs. logical diagrams, rack diagrams, cable maps, asset inventory, SLAs, wireless surveys), lifecycle management (EOL/EOS, decommissioning, firmware and patch management), change management, and configuration management with baselines and backups. Monitoring with SNMP, flow data, packet capture, log aggregation, port mirroring, baseline metrics, and API integration. Disaster recovery: RPO/RTO, cold/warm/hot sites, active-active vs. active-passive, and tabletop/failover testing. Adds remote access: site-to-site and client-to-site VPNs, split tunneling, jump boxes, and in-band vs. out-of-band management.

LESSON 7DOMAIN 4.0 · 14%EXAM 4

Network Security — Threats, Vulnerabilities & Countermeasures

CIA · IAM · Attacks · Hardening · NAC · Segmentation

Security concepts: the CIA triad, encryption in transit and at rest, PKI and certificates, identity and access management (authentication, MFA, SSO, RADIUS, TACACS+, LDAP, SAML), least privilege and role-based access, geofencing, physical security, deception technologies (honeypots/honeynets), and audits and regulatory compliance (data locality, PCI DSS, GDPR). Attack types: DoS/DDoS, VLAN hopping, MAC flooding, ARP poisoning and spoofing, DNS poisoning, rogue devices and evil twins, on-path attacks, social engineering (phishing, tailgating, shoulder surfing), and malware. Defense: device hardening, NAC with 802.1X and port security, key management, ACLs, content filtering, security zones, and segmentation enforcement for IoT/IIoT, SCADA/ICS/OT, guest, and BYOD networks.

Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of LAN 121, students will be able to:

📘 Course Learning Objectives

  1. Describe the history and evolution of data communications and computer networks
  2. Determine hardware and software requirements to operate various types of networks
  3. Describe the various types of data transmission media and topologies
  4. Describe Ethernet media access
  5. Categorize network operations in terms of the OSI model
  6. Discuss various types of protocol stacks and their advantages and disadvantages
  7. Analyze various types of network configurations
  8. Use various TCP/IP utilities to troubleshoot and analyze IP networks
  9. Describe the purpose and characteristics of fault tolerance

🏛️ Program Learning Objectives

  • Install, configure, test, operate, maintain, and manage information systems including networks, network devices, security devices, and mobile devices
  • Install, configure, manage, update, secure, and troubleshoot portable, mobile, and smart devices
  • Participate in information systems equipment procurement, testing, deployment, operations, maintenance, and dispensation of equipment

🌐 Common Learning Objectives

  • Communication: development and expression of ideas to a specific audience
  • Critical Thinking: analysis of an issue in order to form a judgment
  • Diverse Perspectives: exploration of differences
  • Information & Technology Literacy: find, evaluate, and apply information
  • Quantitative Literacy: competency in working with numerical data
