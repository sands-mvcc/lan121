Binary, Hex & Two's Complement — Type Your Answers, Get Graded

Every IPv4 subnet mask, every MAC address, every IPv6 shorthand is number-base arithmetic wearing a network costume. This exercise is a hands-on workout: convert binary to decimal and back, translate binary to hexadecimal and back using the nibble trick, encode negative numbers in 8-bit two's complement, and perform binary addition and subtraction the way the hardware does. Answers are typed, not multiple choice — leading zeros and letter case won't hurt you, but the math has to be right. Grade each task as you go, then grade the whole exercise and download your PDF summary for the LMS. Master this now and Lesson 2's subnetting becomes mental math.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  1. Convert binary numbers to decimal using positional weights (128-64-32-16-8-4-2-1). BLOOM: APPLY
  2. Convert decimal numbers to binary via repeated division or weight subtraction. BLOOM: APPLY
  3. Translate between binary and hexadecimal by grouping bits into 4-bit nibbles. BLOOM: APPLY
  4. Encode and decode negative values using 8-bit two's complement (invert and add one). BLOOM: APPLY
  5. Perform binary addition with carries and subtraction with borrows or two's complement addition. BLOOM: APPLY
  6. Connect each skill to its networking payoff — subnet masks, MAC addresses, and IPv6 notation. BLOOM: UNDERSTAND

4 PTSNOT GRADED

Two's complement is how computers store negative numbers. To encode −N in 8 bits: write +N in 8 bits, invert every bit, add 1. Answers 5.1–5.3 must be exactly 8 bits. In this system the leftmost bit is the sign: 1 means negative.

5.1 · ENCODE−5 =Correct answer: 11111011  (+5=00000101 → invert 11111010 → +1)
5.2 · ENCODE−18 =Correct answer: 11101110  (+18=00010010 → invert 11101101 → +1)
5.3 · ENCODE−1 =Correct answer: 11111111 — negative one is ALL ones, in any width
5.4 · DECODE10011100₂ (two's complement) = decimalCorrect answer: −100  (sign bit is 1 → invert 01100011, +1 = 01100100 = 100, so −100)
5 PTSNOT GRADED

Addition rules: 0+0=0, 0+1=1, 1+1=10 (write 0, carry 1), 1+1+1=11 (write 1, carry 1). For 6.5, subtract by adding the two's complement of the second number and discarding the carry-out — exactly what the CPU does.

6.1 · ADD0101 + 0011 =Correct answer: 1000  (5+3=8; watch the carries ripple)
6.2 · ADD1011 + 0110 =Correct answer: 10001  (11+6=17 — the sum grows a fifth bit)
6.3 · ADD01100110 + 00101101 =Correct answer: 10010011  (102+45=147)
6.4 · SUBTRACT1101 − 0110 =Correct answer: 0111  (13−6=7; borrow twice, or add the complement)
6.5 · SUBTRACT VIA 2's COMPLEMENT01010110 − 00101101 = (8-bit answer, discard the carry)Correct answer: 00101001  (86−45=41: add 11010011, the two's complement of 45, and drop the ninth bit)
🎓Why this matters: "AND the address with the mask" — the heart of subnetting — is bitwise binary math. And hardware has no subtraction circuit at all: it negates with two's complement and adds. You just did arithmetic the way silicon does.
