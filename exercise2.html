One Model, Five Lenses: Map Everything to Its Layer

The OSI model is the filing cabinet the entire Network+ exam is organized around — and the skeleton of every troubleshooting conversation you'll ever have. In this exercise you'll view all seven layers through five professional lenses: the data structures (PDUs) each layer speaks in, the protocols that live at each layer, the core services each layer provides, the devices that operate there, and the security threats that attack there. By the end, "that's a Layer 2 problem" will be something you say — and mean. Grade tasks as you go, then grade the whole exercise, enter your name, and download your PDF summary for the LMS.

🎯

Learning Objectives

  1. Recall the seven OSI layers in order, in both directions, and the encapsulation/de-encapsulation flow. BLOOM: REMEMBER
  2. Match each layer to its protocol data unit — bits, frames, packets, segments/datagrams, and data. BLOOM: REMEMBER
  3. Classify common protocols (HTTP, TLS, TCP, IP, Ethernet, and more) into their home layers. BLOOM: UNDERSTAND
  4. Associate each layer with its core service — from bit transmission to application access. BLOOM: UNDERSTAND
  5. Assign network devices — hubs, switches, routers, and their kin — to the layer at which they make forwarding decisions. BLOOM: ANALYZE
  6. Map security threats to the layer they attack, connecting the OSI model to Domain 4's defense thinking. BLOOM: ANALYZE

4 PTSNOT GRADED
6.1 When a host SENDS data, encapsulation proceeds in which direction?
Answer: Sending = down the stack (7→1), wrapping headers as it goes. Receiving reverses it: de-encapsulation from 1 up to 7, unwrapping at each layer.
6.2 Which mnemonic walks the layers BOTTOM-UP (1→7)?
Answer: Physical-Data Link-Network-Transport-Session-Presentation-Application. "All People Seem To Need Data Processing" is the same stack top-down; "Some People Fear Birthdays" is the Task 1 PDU hook.
6.3 A technician using the BOTTOM-UP troubleshooting strategy starts by checking…
Answer: Bottom-up begins at the physical layer and climbs. This is why the OSI model matters: it's not trivia, it's the search order for Lesson 8's troubleshooting methodology.
6.4 A frame arrives at a receiving host. Which header is processed FIRST?
Answer: Receipt is the mirror of sending: Layer 2's frame header/FCS is checked first, then IP, then TCP, then the application data — 1 up to 7.
