The 6-Step Troubleshooting Methodology

The CompTIA troubleshooting methodology provides a systematic approach to resolving network issues. This structured process ensures thorough investigation and proper documentation.

1

Identify the Problem

  • Gather Information: Talk to users, examine error messages
  • Identify Symptoms: Document what's not working
  • Determine Scope: Single user or network-wide?
  • Question Users: When did it start? What changed?
  • Duplicate the Problem: Can you reproduce it?
2

Establish Theory of Probable Cause

  • Question the Obvious: Check simple solutions first
  • Consider Multiple Approaches:
    • Top-Down (OSI Layer 7 → 1)
    • Bottom-Up (OSI Layer 1 → 7)
    • Divide and Conquer
  • Recent Changes: New hardware, software, or configurations
3

Test Theory to Determine Cause

  • If Theory Confirmed: Determine next steps to resolve
  • If Theory Not Confirmed: Establish new theory or escalate
  • Use Tools: Command-line utilities, monitoring software
  • Document Results: Keep track of what you've tested
4

Establish Plan of Action & Implement Solution

  • Plan Before Acting: Consider potential effects
  • Identify Resources: Tools, time, personnel needed
  • Consider Rollback: How to undo changes if needed
  • Implement: Make the necessary changes
5

Verify System Functionality

  • Test the Fix: Confirm the problem is resolved
  • Full System Check: Ensure no new problems were created
  • User Verification: Have users confirm functionality
  • Preventive Measures: Implement safeguards if applicable
6

Document Findings & Actions

  • Problem Description: What was the issue?
  • Solution Applied: What fixed it?
  • Lessons Learned: How to prevent recurrence
  • Knowledge Base: Update documentation for future reference

💡 Pro Tip: Start Simple, Work Complex

Always begin with the most obvious and simple solutions before moving to complex diagnostics. Many network issues have surprisingly simple causes!

Essential Command-Line Tools

These command-line utilities are fundamental for network troubleshooting and are heavily tested on the CompTIA Network+ exam.

PING - Test Connectivity

Uses ICMP to test reachability and measure round-trip time.

C:\> ping 8.8.8.8
Pinging 8.8.8.8 with 32 bytes of data: Reply from 8.8.8.8: bytes=32 time=14ms TTL=117 Reply from 8.8.8.8: bytes=32 time=13ms TTL=117 Reply from 8.8.8.8: bytes=32 time=12ms TTL=117 Reply from 8.8.8.8: bytes=32 time=15ms TTL=117 Ping statistics for 8.8.8.8: Packets: Sent = 4, Received = 4, Lost = 0 (0% loss), Approximate round trip times in milli-seconds: Minimum = 12ms, Maximum = 15ms, Average = 13ms

Key Parameters:

  • ping -t [host] - Continuous ping
  • ping -n [count] [host] - Specify number of packets
  • ping -l [size] [host] - Specify packet size

IPCONFIG - IP Configuration

Displays and manages TCP/IP network configuration.

C:\> ipconfig /all
Windows IP Configuration Ethernet adapter Local Area Connection: Connection-specific DNS Suffix : company.local Description . . . . . . . . . . : Intel Ethernet Adapter Physical Address. . . . . . . . : 00-1B-21-82-45-CE DHCP Enabled. . . . . . . . . . : Yes IP Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.100 Subnet Mask . . . . . . . . . . : 255.255.255.0 Default Gateway . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1 DHCP Server . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1 DNS Servers . . . . . . . . . . : 8.8.8.8, 8.8.4.4

Key Commands:

  • ipconfig /release - Release IP address
  • ipconfig /renew - Renew IP address
  • ipconfig /flushdns - Clear DNS cache
  • ipconfig /displaydns - Display DNS cache

TRACERT - Route Tracing

Shows the path packets take to reach a destination.

C:\> tracert google.com
Tracing route to google.com [142.250.190.78] over a maximum of 30 hops: 1 1 ms 1 ms 1 ms 192.168.1.1 2 12 ms 11 ms 10 ms 10.0.0.1 3 15 ms 14 ms 16 ms isp-gateway.net [203.0.113.1] 4 22 ms 21 ms 20 ms core1.isp.net [203.0.113.10] 5 28 ms 27 ms 29 ms google-peer.net [142.250.190.78] Trace complete.

Analysis Points:

  • Each line represents a router hop
  • Three time measurements show latency
  • * * * indicates packet loss or filtering
  • High latency spikes indicate bottlenecks

NSLOOKUP - DNS Troubleshooting

Query DNS servers for name resolution information.

C:\> nslookup www.example.com
Server: dns.company.local Address: 192.168.1.1 Non-authoritative answer: Name: www.example.com Address: 93.184.216.34

Advanced Usage:

  • nslookup -type=MX domain.com - Mail exchange records
  • nslookup -type=NS domain.com - Name server records
  • nslookup IP-address - Reverse DNS lookup

NETSTAT - Network Statistics

Display active network connections and listening ports.

C:\> netstat -an
Active Connections Proto Local Address Foreign Address State TCP 0.0.0.0:22 0.0.0.0:0 LISTENING TCP 0.0.0.0:80 0.0.0.0:0 LISTENING TCP 192.168.1.100:443 173.252.74.22:80 ESTABLISHED TCP 192.168.1.100:1234 52.97.128.18:443 ESTABLISHED UDP 0.0.0.0:53 *:*

Useful Parameters:

  • netstat -a - All connections and listening ports
  • netstat -n - Numerical addresses instead of resolving hosts
  • netstat -b - Show executable associated with each connection
  • netstat -r - Display routing table

Practical Troubleshooting Examples

🔧 Scenario 1: User Cannot Access Internet

Problem: A user reports they cannot browse websites or send emails.

Step-by-Step Resolution:

Step 1: Identify the Problem
  • Question user: When did it start? What exactly doesn't work?
  • Determine scope: Just this user or multiple users?
  • Check error messages: "Page cannot be displayed" etc.
Step 2: Theory of Probable Cause
  • Physical connectivity issue
  • IP configuration problem
  • DNS resolution failure
  • Gateway/routing issue
Step 3: Test the Theory
ipconfig /all

Check if IP address is assigned (not 169.254.x.x APIPA)

ping 127.0.0.1

Test local TCP/IP stack

ping 192.168.1.1

Test connectivity to default gateway

ping 8.8.8.8

Test internet connectivity (bypassing DNS)

nslookup google.com

Test DNS resolution

🔧 Scenario 2: Slow Network Performance

Problem: Users complain that network access is very slow.

Diagnostic Commands:
ping -t google.com

Check for packet loss and latency variations

tracert www.slowsite.com

Identify where delays occur in the network path

netstat -e

Check for interface errors and utilization

Analysis Points:
  • High Latency (>100ms): Network congestion or routing issues
  • Packet Loss: Hardware problems or network overload
  • Specific Hop Delays: Problem with particular router/ISP
  • Interface Errors: Physical layer problems

🔧 Scenario 3: Cannot Access Specific Website

Problem: User can browse most sites but cannot access company intranet.

Troubleshooting Approach:
nslookup intranet.company.com

Verify DNS can resolve the hostname

ping intranet.company.com

Test basic connectivity to resolved IP

telnet intranet.company.com 80

Test specific port connectivity

tracert intranet.company.com

Check routing path to destination
