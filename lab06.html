Wireless Network Configuration Labs

Secure a small-office WLAN with WPA2-Personal, then rebuild it as WPA2-Enterprise with RADIUS.

What a wireless network specialist needs to know and do

This job-task analysis describes the knowledge and performance skills of an IT specialist who configures and manages enterprise wireless networks. The two labs and the knowledge check practice a focused subset of it. Content aligns with CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) Domains 2.0 Network Implementation, 4.0 Network Security and 5.0 Network Troubleshooting; verify against the current published objectives before mapping to a syllabus.

Knowledge

RF and 802.11 fundamentals
  • 802.11 amendments and generations: a/b/g/n (Wi-Fi 4), ac (Wi-Fi 5), ax (Wi-Fi 6/6E), be (Wi-Fi 7), and their bands, rates and channel widths.
  • 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz bands: range versus throughput, and why 1, 6 and 11 are the only non-overlapping 2.4 GHz channels in North America.
  • Co-channel and adjacent-channel interference, channel bonding, DFS channels and transmit power.
  • SSID, BSSID, BSS/ESS, roaming, and signal metrics (RSSI, SNR, noise floor).
  • Antenna types (omni, directional), placement, and attenuation from building materials.
Wireless security protocols and threats
  • Evolution from WEP and WPA (TKIP) to WPA2 (AES-CCMP) and WPA3 (SAE, GCMP, 192-bit mode).
  • Personal (pre-shared key) versus Enterprise (802.1X) authentication, and when each is appropriate.
  • The WPA2 4-way handshake: PMK, PTK and GTK, and why a weak passphrase allows offline dictionary attacks.
  • Attacks: WPS PIN brute force, evil twin and rogue APs, deauthentication, KRACK, and passphrase sharing after staff leave.
  • Guest networks, client isolation, captive portals and MAC filtering (and its limits).
AAA, 802.1X and RADIUS
  • 802.1X roles: supplicant (client), authenticator (AP or controller), authentication server (RADIUS).
  • EAP methods: PEAP (EAP-MSCHAPv2), EAP-TLS, EAP-TTLS and EAP-FAST, and which need client certificates.
  • RADIUS: UDP 1812 authentication and 1813 accounting (legacy 1645/1646), shared secrets, Access-Request, -Challenge, -Accept and -Reject.
  • Network policies and conditions (group, NAS port type), directory integration (Active Directory, LDAP) and dynamic VLAN assignment.
  • RADIUS versus TACACS+, and the value of accounting records for audit and incident response.
Certificates and PKI
  • Certificate authorities, trust chains and distributing a root CA to managed clients.
  • Why supplicants must validate the RADIUS server certificate and name to defeat evil-twin credential theft.
  • Certificate lifecycle: expiration, renewal and revocation, and how an expired server certificate takes down Wi-Fi for everyone.
Enterprise WLAN architecture and operations
  • Autonomous versus controller-based (lightweight) and cloud-managed APs; CAPWAP tunnels.
  • PoE budgets, VLAN-per-SSID design, and segmenting guest, staff and IoT traffic.
  • Site surveys (predictive, passive, active), heat maps and capacity planning.
  • Monitoring with SNMP, syslog and WIPS; firmware management and configuration backups.

Performance skills

Plan
  • Interpret a site survey and select bands and channels that avoid interference.
  • Choose the authentication model (PSK or 802.1X) from organizational size, turnover and audit requirements.
  • Define an addressing and DHCP plan for wireless clients.
Configure
  • Harden a SOHO router: unique admin password, remote management off, WPS off.
  • Configure SSID, security mode, cipher and a strong passphrase.
  • Register an AP as a RADIUS client with a strong shared secret.
  • Build a RADIUS network policy: group condition, NAS port type, EAP method and server certificate.
  • Configure an AP for WPA2-Enterprise with authentication and accounting servers.
  • Configure supplicants for PEAP with server certificate validation.
Verify and troubleshoot
  • Confirm addressing and reachability with ipconfig and ping.
  • Read the EAP/RADIUS exchange to locate where authentication failed.
  • Diagnose common faults: wrong key, cipher mismatch, APIPA (169.254.x.x) addresses, shared-secret mismatch, untrusted or expired certificate, and policy denial.
  • Use RADIUS accounting logs to confirm who connected, from where and when.
Document and manage
  • Record configurations in a work order or change ticket, without storing secrets in plain text.
  • Apply least privilege to group membership and remove access when people leave.
  • Explain security trade-offs to a non-technical client.

        Instructor notes

        Learning objectives

        After completing the activity, students will be able to:

        • Configure a SOHO wireless router with WPA2-Personal (AES), a strong passphrase, a survey-based channel, DHCP and hardened administration.
        • Connect and verify two wireless clients using DHCP addressing and ICMP reachability.
        • Configure a RADIUS server with a RADIUS client entry, least-privilege group membership and a PEAP network policy.
        • Configure an access point for WPA2-Enterprise using RADIUS authentication and accounting.
        • Configure 802.1X supplicants with server certificate validation and interpret the EAP exchange to locate faults.
        Logistics
        • Time: Lab 1 about 40 minutes, Lab 2 about 60 minutes, knowledge check 15 minutes.
        • Prerequisites: IPv4 addressing and DHCP; the CIA triad; basic symmetric encryption.
        • Delivery: individual or pairs (one plays the client, one the technician, swapping for Lab 2). Works in class or asynchronously; progress is saved in the browser.
        • Suggested sequence: brief on PSK versus 802.1X, Lab 1, debrief on why PSK fails at scale, Lab 2, knowledge check, reflection.
