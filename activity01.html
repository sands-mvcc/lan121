Deep Analysis: Know the Exam Before You Study for It

Professionals don't study blind — they profile the exam first. In this exercise you'll dissect the CompTIA Network+ N10-009: its structure, domains and weights, question formats, scoring, and where it sits in the certification pathway. You'll use the official resources below to hunt down facts, classify real objective topics into their domains, take apart the anatomy of an exam question, and build a study plan whose hours actually match the exam's weighting. Every task is auto-graded; grade tasks individually as you go, then grade the whole exercise at the bottom and record your score in the LMS.

5 PTSNOT GRADED

Hunt these facts down on CompTIA's official Network+ page. No guessing — verify.

2.1 What is the current Network+ exam code?
Answer: N10-009 (launched June 2024). N10-008 is its retired predecessor; SY0-701 is Security+, 220-1101 is A+.
2.2 What is the MAXIMUM number of questions on the exam?
Answer: Up to 90 questions — and note "maximum": your exam may present fewer.
2.3 How much time do you get?
Answer: 90 minutes — with up to 90 questions, budget roughly a minute each, banking time early for the PBQs.
2.4 What is the passing score?
Answer: 720 on CompTIA's 100–900 scale — a scaled score, not a straight percentage. (675 is Security+'s bar.)
2.5 Which question formats should you expect?
Answer: MCQs plus PBQs — interactive simulations like dragging cables, configuring a switch, or ordering troubleshooting steps. Task 4 dissects the MCQ side.

3 PTSNOT GRADED

Read this exam-style item like a test designer, then answer the three analysis questions.

🧪SAMPLE ITEM: "A technician replaces a switch. Afterward, a server's link shows up/up, but users report the connection is extremely slow, and the interface counters show increasing late collisions and CRC errors. Which of the following is the MOST likely cause?  (A) DNS misconfiguration  (B) Duplex mismatch  (C) DHCP scope exhaustion  (D) Invalid SSL certificate"
4.1 What is the correct answer to the sample item?
Answer: Up/up + slow + late collisions + CRC is the duplex mismatch signature. DNS/DHCP/SSL failures don't produce Layer 1/2 error counters.
4.2 Which domain is this item testing?
Answer: A symptom set plus "MOST likely cause" is Domain 5's signature move — and Domain 5 is the exam's biggest slice at 24%.
4.3 The distractors (A, C, D) share a design pattern. What is it?
Answer: Good distractors are real and familiar — DNS, DHCP, SSL — but their failure modes (name resolution, addressing, certificates) can't produce these Layer 1/2 counters. Match SYMPTOMS to failure modes, not vocabulary to vocabulary. Also notice the qualifier "MOST likely": several answers may be possible; one best fits the evidence.
