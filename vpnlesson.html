VPN Lessons

Purpose of VPNs

Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) provide a secure connection between remote users or sites and a private network over the internet.
Main purposes include:

  • Privacy: VPNs encrypt traffic, hiding user data from eavesdropping and ISPs.
  • Security: VPNs secure sensitive business or personal data from cyber threats, especially on public Wi-Fi.
  • Remote Access: Employees can access their organization’s internal resources securely from anywhere.
  • Bypass Restrictions: Users can access geo-restricted content or bypass censorship by routing traffic through remote servers.
Example: A company’s employee works from home and connects to the corporate network securely using a VPN, ensuring confidential files are protected.

Types of VPNs

  • Remote Access VPN
    Connects individual users to a private network from anywhere via encrypted tunnels.
    Use case: Remote workers connecting securely to their office resources.
  • Site-to-Site VPN (Router-to-Router)
    Connects entire office networks in different locations over the internet.
    Use case: Two company branches securely sharing internal data.
  • Client-to-Site (SSL VPN)
    Uses a web browser or app for users to connect to internal web resources.
    Example: Employees accessing internal applications through a secure portal.
More: Large organizations may use Hybrid VPNs, combining both Remote Access and Site-to-Site VPNs.

VPN Protocols

  • IPSec (Internet Protocol Security): Used for both Site-to-Site and Remote Access VPNs. Provides strong encryption and authentication.
  • OpenVPN: Open-source, highly secure, and flexible. Uses SSL/TLS for key exchange. Supports most operating systems.
  • L2TP/IPSec (Layer 2 Tunneling Protocol): Combines L2TP and IPSec for enhanced security. Frequently used in enterprise VPNs.
  • PPTP (Point-to-Point Tunneling Protocol): One of the oldest protocols. Fast, but considered weak in security.
  • WireGuard: Modern, lightweight, and fast protocol. Gaining popularity for its simplicity and security.
Tip: OpenVPN and WireGuard are widely used today, as they balance strong security with speed.

VPN Configuration Options

  • Server Address: The VPN gateway's IP or domain name to connect to.
  • Authentication: Passwords, pre-shared keys (PSK), or certificates for verifying identity.
  • Encryption Algorithm: Choice of cipher (e.g., AES-256, ChaCha20) for securing data.
  • Tunneling Protocol: Protocol selected (see Protocols tab) for encapsulating traffic.
  • Split Tunnel or Full Tunnel: Choose whether to route all traffic or only specific resources through the VPN.
  • Client Settings: Software/app and OS compatibility, auto-connect options.
Common Setup: A VPN may require the user to enter the server address, a username, and a pre-shared key, then select the protocol (like IPSec), and encryption level before making a secure connection.
